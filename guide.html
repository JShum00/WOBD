How to use WOBD

Everything you need to go from "my check engine light is on" to a plain-English answer.

1. What you need

  • A car with OBD-II. In the US, that's every car and light truck from model year 1996 on.
  • A USB ELM327-compatible adapter. See the next section.
  • A laptop or Chromebook running Chrome, Edge, or Opera. Phones and tablets aren't supported. WOBD needs an HTTPS connection (or localhost when running it locally).

2. How to get an adapter

OBD-II adapters are sold at auto parts stores and online. WOBD connects to a USB adapter through your computer's serial port. When you shop:

  • Buy a USB cable adapter, not Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.
  • Check that it says ELM327-compatible.

WOBD checks the adapter's baud rate automatically. If it doesn't get an answer, you can choose a speed and retry. See the Tested Adapters page for personal test results; an untested adapter may or may not work.

3. Finding the OBD port

The port is under the dashboard on the driver's side, within reach of the steering wheel. It's a 16-pin, trapezoid-shaped plug, sometimes behind a small cover.

4. Using the site

  1. Plug the adapter into the car and into your computer.
  2. Plug the adapter into the car's OBD-II port and your computer's USB port.
  3. Turn the ignition to On. The engine can stay off for a code scan.
  4. Open WOBD and click Connect. Choose your adapter in the browser's device picker and allow the connection.
  5. Wait while WOBD detects the adapter speed and talks to the car. Older cars can take a few seconds. If detection fails, choose a baud rate in Bob's message and click Retry; click Cancel to stop detection.
  6. Enter the car's year, make, and model at the top, or click Skip. These details are optional and only personalize the external code-search link and report.
  7. Click Scan. The summary shows the check-engine-light state when available and the stored code count.
  8. Select a code to see Bob's explanation, urgency, common causes, and what to tell a mechanic. Generic database entries are labeled as general information.
  9. Use Print report to take the results to a shop. Choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog to keep a copy; the report includes the scan details and VIN when the car provides one.

After connecting, the connection panel shows the protocol, whether the car is responding, and adapter voltage. Open More info for adapter identification and advanced details. The adapter's reported version can suggest that it is a clone, but WOBD can't verify authenticity.

5. Live data

With the adapter connected, open the Live data tab to see real-time readings. WOBD asks the car which readings it supports and shows those gauges, plus adapter voltage. Some cars report only a few readings. Only watch live data while parked or from the passenger seat.

  • Engine RPM: how fast the engine is turning. A warm engine usually idles between 600 and 900.
  • Speed: the speed the car's computer reports, in miles per hour.
  • Coolant and intake-air temperature: temperatures reported by the engine computer.
  • Engine load and throttle: percentages reported by the car.
  • Fuel trims: how the computer is adjusting fuel delivery.
  • Airflow, manifold pressure, timing, and oxygen sensors: readings appear when the car reports support for them.
  • Fuel system: open- or closed-loop status, when supported.
  • Battery: adapter voltage. About 12.4 to 12.7 V with the engine off, and 13.5 to 14.7 V running.

When Bob's read-aloud control is available, you can turn it on to hear his speech bubble. It uses your browser's speech synthesis and remembers your choice on this device.

6. Clearing codes

Clear stored codes after a repair. WOBD asks you to confirm that the ignition is on and the engine is off before sending the clear command. If the problem isn't fixed, the warning light or code may return.

Don't clear codes right before an emissions inspection. Clearing also resets the car's readiness monitors, and the car has to be driven for a while before it's ready to pass again.

7. Troubleshooting

  • The adapter doesn't appear in the browser's device picker: unplug and reseat the cable, try another USB port, then click Connect again.
  • Baud detection gets no answer: confirm that the cable is ELM327-compatible, then choose a baud rate in Bob's retry form. You can cancel and reconnect to start over.
  • The adapter connects but the car doesn't respond: turn the ignition to On, check that the adapter is fully seated, and try again. Some older vehicles take longer to respond.
  • WOBD says the browser can't connect: use Chrome, Edge, or Opera on a laptop or Chromebook, and open the site over HTTPS (or localhost during local development). Phones and tablets aren't supported.

8. Your data

Scans run in your browser and aren't uploaded. The app shell and code databases are cached for offline use after the first visit. If you choose a Google search link, that search opens outside WOBD and includes the code and any vehicle details you entered.

WOBD gives general information, not a professional diagnosis. Always confirm a problem before replacing parts.
