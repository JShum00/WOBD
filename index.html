    Free OBD-II scanner in your browser
    
    
    WOBD reads your car's check engine light codes and live data through a USB OBD-II adapter and explains them in plain English: how urgent each code is, what usually causes it, and what to tell your mechanic. There's nothing to install and it's free. Not sure what a code means? Try Code Search or the how-to guide.
    
    
    Frequently asked questions
    
    
    What do I need to use WOBD?
    A car with OBD-II (US model year 1996 or newer), a USB ELM327-compatible adapter, and a laptop or Chromebook running Chrome, Edge, or Opera.
    
    
    Does WOBD work on a phone?
    No. WOBD uses Web Serial, which phones and tablets don't support. Use a laptop or Chromebook.
    
    
    Is my data private?
    Yes. Scan data stays in your browser. Only a search you choose to open, or a VIN lookup you agree to, leaves the site.
    
    
    Can I look up a code without an adapter?
    Yes. Code Search explains trouble codes offline, with no adapter or car needed.
    
    
    What is WOBD?
    WOBD is a free OBD-II scanner that runs in your browser. It gives general information, not a professional diagnosis, so always confirm a problem before replacing parts.

    This turns off the check engine light, but it also resets your car's emissions readiness monitors.
    
          
    If you're going for an emissions inspection soon, don't clear. The car has to drive for a while before it's ready again, and it can fail until then.
    
        
    
      
    
      
      
    
        
        
      
    
    
    
      

  
    
    
      
    Look up your car from its VIN?
    
      
    Your car reported its VIN. WOBD can send it to NHTSA's free public VIN decoder (vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov) to fill in your year, make, and model.
    
      
    The VIN leaves your device for this lookup. WOBD doesn't save it. If you'd rather not, pick your car from the lists instead.

    Record undecoded parameters
    
      
    WOBD can save raw responses from parameters it cannot currently interpret.
    
      
    Some parameters may have known names, while others may be unidentified. Their values and units cannot be reliably displayed until an appropriate decoder is available.
    
      
    Recording additional parameters may slightly reduce live-data refresh rates.
